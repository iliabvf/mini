Sam had a blue kite. He held the string with both hands and ran across the grass. The wind was strong. The kite climbed over the fence and up into the sky. Mini stood by the gate and watched the kite dance.

The wind pulled harder. The string slipped from Sam's hand. The kite flew to the old tree at the end of the field. It caught on a high branch and the string hung down like a thin rope. Sam stopped running. He looked up and did not know how to reach it.

Mini walked to the tree. The lowest branch was close to the ground. Mini held it still while Sam climbed. Sam moved slowly from branch to branch. Leaves brushed his face. A small bird hopped away and watched from the next tree. Sam reached the kite and lifted it free. The paper was bent, but the color was still a clear blue.

They climbed down together. Sam held the kite against his chest. Mini wound the string into a neat loop. They sat on the grass and ate a red apple each. Sam said the kite would fly again after he mended the bent corner. The wind grew quiet. The field was green and the sky was blue.

In the evening they walked home along the path. Sam carried the kite. Mini carried the apples that were left. A dog barked once from a yard and then lay down. Lights came on in the windows. Sam put the kite on a chair by the door so he would see it in the morning.

The next day the wind was gentle. Sam mended the corner with a small piece of paper. Mini held the string. They walked back to the same field. This time Sam did not let go. The kite rose, steadied, and stayed in the air. Sam laughed. Mini counted the turns of the string. One, two, three. The kite was blue against the sky, and it did not go into the tree.

Pip is a small cat. She likes warm places. In the morning she jumps onto the desk and sits beside the computer. The computer is warm, so she folds her paws and shuts her eyes. Mini works at the desk and types short lines. Pip does not read them. She listens to the quiet sound of the keys.

At noon Mini pours water into a white bowl. Pip drinks, then looks at the window. Birds sit on the wire outside. Pip's ears turn toward them, but she stays on the desk. She knows the window is shut. The birds are safe, and so is she. After drinking she washes her face with one paw.

In the afternoon the sun moves across the floor. Pip follows the patch of light. She sleeps there until the light reaches the wall. Then she walks to the kitchen and sits by her bowl, even when the bowl is full. Mini says her name. Pip answers with a short sound and does not move until the bowl is set down again.

In the evening Pip climbs onto the chair by the door. She watches the path outside. Sometimes the dog from the yard walks by. Pip does not hiss. She only watches, her tail still. When the house is quiet she returns to the desk, curls up, and sleeps. Mini turns off the computer. The room goes dark, and the cat stays warm.

On Sunday Mini and Sam walked to the river. The path was narrow and lined with tall grass. They counted stones as they went. Sam found a white stone, a gray stone, and a stone with a stripe. Mini found a flat stone that was good for skipping. They kept the flat stone in a pocket.

The river was slow. It shone where the sun touched it. Sam threw the flat stone. It skipped once, then twice, then sank. Mini tried next. The stone skipped three times. They both laughed at the splash. A duck swam past and did not care about the stones.

They sat on a log and shared bread. Sam asked what the water was. Mini said water is a clear drink, and the river is water moving to the sea. They did not drink from the river. They drank from a bottle they had carried from home. The bread was simple and good.

Clouds gathered, but no rain fell. They walked back before the light faded. Sam carried the striped stone. Mini carried nothing but the memory of the three skips. At the gate they saw the old tree where the kite had stuck. The branch was empty. The kite was at home on the chair. Pip was in the window, watching them come up the path.

Sam opened the door. Pip jumped down and rubbed against his leg. Mini set the bottle on the table. They told the story of the river to the quiet room. The kite was blue. The stone was striped. The cat was named Pip. Outside, the wind moved the grass, and inside, everyone was home.

On Monday Mini and Sam went to the market. The road was bright and the air was cool. Sam carried a small bag. Mini carried a list. Pip stayed home on the desk, in the warm square of sun, and did not ask to come.

The market was a row of tables under a long cloth roof. A woman sold red apples in a wooden box. Sam picked four apples and put them in the bag. Mini paid with coins and said thank you. Next to the apples was a table of bread. The loaves were round and brown. They bought one loaf to share on the way home.

A boy at the next table had a blue kite, smaller than Sam's kite. Sam stopped to look. He told the boy about the tree, the climb, and the mended corner. The boy nodded. His kite had a white tail. The wind in the market was too weak to fly, so the kite stayed on the table.

They walked past a bowl of water where a man kept smooth stones. Mini saw a striped stone like the one from the river. She did not buy it. Sam already had a striped stone at home. They laughed and moved on. A dog slept under the table and did not wake.

Before noon the bag was full enough. They turned back toward the path. Sam ate one apple as they walked. It was sweet. Mini carried the bread. They counted the gates they passed. One, two, three. At their own gate Pip was in the window, watching, just as she had watched them come home from the river.

Inside, Mini set the apples in a bowl and the bread on the board. Sam put the kite on its chair so the blue paper could be seen. Pip jumped down, smelled the bag, and went back to the desk. The room was quiet. The market was over, and the day was still young.
